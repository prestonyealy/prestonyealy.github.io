Backyard Trebuchet

A recreational and educational trebuchet developed as a complete SolidWorks assembly with fabrication drawings, an exploded view, a bill of materials, and a gravity-driven motion study.

Mission

Design a mechanism that could be built from accessible materials.

The project translated a counterweight trebuchet concept into a documented mechanical assembly for recreational use and classroom demonstrations of motion and energy transfer.

The frame and bucket were specified in pine for availability and ease of fabrication. Plain carbon steel was selected for axles and fasteners to provide strength and wear resistance, while a plywood launch surface supported the projectile path.

Engineering workflow

What the project demonstrates.

Assembly modeling

Created a multi-part SolidWorks assembly to control interfaces among the base, frame, throwing arm, bucket, axles, slide, and fasteners.

Design documentation

Produced an exploded view, BOM, alternate-position drawing, and individual drawings for the modeled components.

Material selection

Balanced accessibility and function by pairing hardware-store pine and plywood with carbon-steel axles and screws.

Motion analysis

Applied gravity in a SolidWorks motion study to check that the rigid assembly rotated smoothly about its pivot without modeled interference.

Tradeoff

Model the rigid mechanism; acknowledge the flexible-system limit.

The sling, rope, and counterweight were intentionally excluded from the digital assembly. Early attempts using 3D sketches and splines did not produce a practical, dimensionable flexible-rope model inside the assembly.

Engineering judgment: the project preserved a useful rigid-body model and documented the limitation instead of presenting an incomplete flexible-element simulation as a validated launch model.

Next iteration

Close the loop with analysis and physical test.

Recommended next version: add a flexible-body or multibody model for the sling and counterweight, perform stress analysis on critical frame and axle components, define a safe payload range, fabricate a prototype, and compare measured launch behavior against the model.
